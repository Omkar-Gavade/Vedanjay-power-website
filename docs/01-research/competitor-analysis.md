# Competitor & Industry Analysis

**Researched:** 2026-08-31
**Method:** live page fetch and DOM inspection of each site's homepage and key inner pages.
Figures quoted are as published by each company on the date of research.

---

## 0. The framing correction that drives everything

The brief names five comparators: Waaree, Tata Power, Vikram Solar, ReNew, Suzlon.

Researching them produced the most important strategic finding of this phase:

> **None of the five is a competitor of Vedanjay Power. Four of the five are Vedanjay's clients.**

Cross-referencing the legacy site's own client list and project register:

| Named "competitor" | Actual relationship to Vedanjay |
|---|---|
| Waaree Energies | **Client** — 3 CEIG approval mandates (Maharashtra Seamless, Growels Mall, Mumbai Metro) |
| Tata Power (Tata Power Solar) | **Client** — 4 mandates incl. CEIG approval for Gabriel Dewas, TCS net-metering |
| Vikram Solar | **Client** — 4 civil infrastructure works (MCR/LCR, water tank, transformer foundation, entrance gate) |
| Suzlon | **Client** — 7 mandates (telemetry, AMR metering, SLDC liaison for 29.4 MW wind, AMC, spares) |
| ReNew | **Client** — 5 O&M mandates (52 km + 18 km 33 kV transmission line O&M, 405 kWp solar O&M) |

These are **asset owners and manufacturers**. They own generation, they sell modules and turbines,
they raise public capital, they report GW-scale portfolios. Vedanjay is a **regulatory, technical
and O&M services firm** that these companies hire to get their assets approved, connected,
metered, scheduled and maintained.

**Consequence for this project.** These five sites are the right places to study *craft* — navigation,
motion, editorial layout, statistics presentation — and the **wrong places to copy information
architecture from**. A product catalogue, an investor-relations tree, a GW-capacity hero and an ESG
report library are correct for an IPP and actively wrong for a consultancy. Vedanjay copying that IA
would produce a site that oversells scale it does not have and undersells expertise it does have.

Therefore this analysis covers two groups:

- **Group A — Sector craft references** (the five named): what to learn about execution.
- **Group B — Structural references**: professional/technical services firms whose *IA* is the right
  model, led by DNV, the closest true analogue in the world to what Vedanjay does.

# Group A — Sector craft references

## A1. Tata Power — `tatapower.com`

**Branding.** Institutional heritage plays hard: "109 years", a memorial tribute to Ratan Tata on the
homepage. Positioning is emotional and national, not technical. Tagline: *"Lighting a billion smiles.
Now and forever."*

**Navigation.** Energy Solutions, News and Media, Investor Resource Center, Careers, Community.
Conventional corporate tree; the Investor Resource Center is deep (annual reports, quarterly reports,
subsidiary financials, investor presentations).

**Homepage sequence (15 sections).** Hero banner → Smart Energy Solutions (horizontal scroll) →
Ratan Tata tribute → media highlights carousel → heritage & sustainability → "Our Squad" (people) →
Carbon Net Zero 2045 → EnerUni / AI initiatives → Investor Hub with live financial charts →
transformation stories → YouTube library → careers → heritage → next-gen energy → community
initiatives.

**Statistics.** 7,856 MW clean energy portfolio · 109 years · ~2 lakh kg carbon reduced ·
7.5 lakh EV charging sessions (50,000 tonnes CO₂ avoided) · 166 offices across 16 states ·
550 functions · 40,000 women reached.

**Interaction.** Horizontal-scroll solution rail; carousels; live-data investor charts.

**Read.** *Emotional scale.* Tata sells belonging and permanence. Note the length — 15 homepage
sections is a conglomerate's problem, not Vedanjay's.

**Take:** the **horizontal-scroll solutions rail** (efficient for 5–6 peer items) and the discipline of
attaching a human or civic outcome to every statistic.
**Avoid:** 15-section homepage; emotional-national register; investor-hub complexity.

## A2. ReNew — `renew.com`

The most sophisticated site of the five, and the clearest IA.

**Navigation.** Six items only: **About Us · Decarbonisation Solutions · Leading Sustainably ·
Investor Relations · Work With Us · RE-Insights**, plus a distinct secondary **Business Enquiries**
action. Each opens into structured subsections (Decarbonisation Solutions → Energy Generation, Net
Zero Solutions for Corporates, Manufacturing, Transmission).

**Homepage sequence.** Hero ("The Future of Energy" / *"Shaping a fossil-free world by accelerating
the Net-Zero transition"*, CTA "Read More", rotating announcement ticker) → **Our Green Footprint**
(statistics) → Decarbonisation Solutions → founder introduction (Sumant Sinha) → press coverage →
FAQs → careers → footer.

**Statistics.** 22,185 GWh clean energy (FY25) · 18.6 Mt GHG avoided · **9% of India's renewable
generation** · 1.7 m lives impacted · 540,372+ m³ water saved · Nasdaq-listed 2021.

**Read.** Three things are exemplary. **(1)** Six nav items, each a *user intent* rather than an org
chart — "Work With Us", "Leading Sustainably" and "Business Enquiries" are how visitors think, not
how the company is structured. **(2)** The framed statistic — *9% of India's renewable energy
generation* — converts a raw number into a claim about national position. **(3)** Statistics carry
an explicit period (FY25), which makes them credible and maintainable.

**Take (highest-value site in this set):** intent-named navigation; a **named** statistics section;
period-stamped figures; a separate enquiry action distinct from nav links; a founder presence on the
homepage.
**Avoid:** the rotating hero announcement ticker (see Group A anti-patterns).

---

## A3. Suzlon — `suzlon.com`

**Navigation.** About Us · Our Businesses · Investors · Sustainability · Media · Careers · Contact Us.

**Homepage.** Notably, **no conventional hero** — the page opens directly into "Our Offerings", five
service cards (Wind, RE DevCo, Storage, RE Asset Management, Full Stack RE). Then: key statistics →
latest news (6 releases) → global presence map → client logo grid → investor section
("Green business is good business") → sustainability → careers CTA.

**Statistics.** 22,000+ MW capacity · 10,000+ operating assets · **1,900+ industrial clients** ·
17 countries.

**Read.** The most interesting decision in the group: **leading with capability instead of a hero
image.** For a company whose buyers already know who it is, a stock landscape wastes the first
screen. It is confident and it is fast. Also note **1,900+ industrial clients** — a *count of clients*
is a services-firm proof, not an asset-owner proof, and it is the single most transferable metric on
any of the five sites.

**Take:** capability-first opening; the client-count statistic; global-presence map as a credibility
device (Vedanjay's equivalent: a **state coverage map** of MP / MH / TG).
**Avoid:** nothing significant — this is the most structurally disciplined of the five.

---

## A4. Waaree Energies — `waaree.com`

**Navigation.** Deep product mega-menu: Company · Group Business · Product (PV Modules → HJT, N-Type,
Mono PERC, Flexible, Special; Inverter → Single/Three Phase; Solar Kits; BESS; Electrolyzer Stacks;
Smart Meter; Waaree Lights; Transformer) · Solutions (Rooftop, Flexible, Special, Storage, Green
Hydrogen) · Investor · People · Network · DMS. A **live stock ticker sits in the navigation bar**
(observed: WAAREEENER ₹2,683.80).

**Hero.** H1: *"Accelerating Global Energy Transition."* Then "Smart energy solutions for every need,
every scale", split **By Product** / by application.

**Statistics.** 25.8 GW · 5.4 GW · 24+ (countries).

**Read.** A textbook **product-led** IA — correct for a manufacturer with a SKU catalogue, and
**exactly the model Vedanjay must not follow.** Vedanjay has no SKUs. The transferable idea is the
**dual-axis entry** (by product *and* by application), whose services equivalent is *by service* and
*by who you are*.

**Take:** dual-axis entry into the offer.
**Avoid:** product-catalogue depth; the nav stock ticker (Vedanjay is unlisted); "Accelerating Global
Energy Transition" — a headline so generic it would fit any of the five.

---

## A5. Vikram Solar — `vikramsolar.com`

**Navigation.** Modules · Solutions · Explore · Company · Investors · Newsroom · Vikicare ·
Contact Us · VS Global.

**Hero.** A **rotating carousel**: "ENABLING CO2 EMISSION REDUCTION" / "CREATING CLIMATE FOR CHANGE",
CTAs "learn more".

**Homepage sequence.** Hero carousel → "Enabling a Green Future" → "Restoring Climate, One Project at
a Time" (EPC) → **"Our Revolution in Numbers"** → Vikram Solar Foundation → footer.

**Statistics.** 39 countries · 1,396 MW O&M ongoing · **1.41 GW aggregate EPC capacity
(as of 31 March 2025)**.

**Read.** Weakest of the five. The hero is a carousel of slogans with a soft "learn more" — the
visitor is told a mood, not a fact. Two consecutive climate-slogan sections say nearly the same
thing. **But one detail is best-in-class:** *"as of March 31, 2025"* attached to the EPC figure.
That single date is the difference between a marketing number and an auditable one, and it is
precisely what Vedanjay's contradictory statistics need.

**Take:** the dated statistic — adopt as a hard rule.
**Avoid:** slogan-carousel hero; duplicated thematic sections; nine top-level nav items.

## Group A anti-patterns — observed, to be avoided

| Anti-pattern | Seen at | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Rotating hero carousel / slogan slider | Vikram Solar, ReNew (ticker), legacy Vedanjay | Visitors read slide 1 and scroll. Slides 2–5 are typically seen by <10% of visitors. Every slide dilutes the first. |
| Scrolling announcement ticker under the nav | ReNew, legacy Vedanjay | Motion near the top competes with the headline; clipped text at edges; an accessibility problem (non-pausable motion). |
| Generic transition slogans as H1 | Waaree, Vikram Solar | "Accelerating Global Energy Transition" is interchangeable across the entire sector — it says nothing only this company could say. |
| Undated statistics | Waaree, Suzlon, Tata Power | An undated number is unfalsifiable and reads as marketing. |
| Homepage as sitemap (15 sections) | Tata Power | Nothing is emphasised when everything is included. |
| Logo wall with no context | All five, legacy Vedanjay | A logo proves contact, not outcome. |

# Group B — Structural references (the correct IA model)

## B1. DNV — `dnv.com/energy` — **the closest analogue that exists**

DNV is an independent energy assurance and advisory firm: technical due diligence, independent
engineer mandates, grid-code compliance, energy market regulation and compliance certification.
Strip the scale (6,000+ energy experts, ~30 years of renewables due diligence, 30+ country sites) and
the *shape of the business is Vedanjay's*: expert services sold to asset owners and financiers, where
the product is judgement and regulatory access.

**IA observed.** Services-led and deeply granular. Breadcrumb: *Energy → Our services for the energy
sector → Advisory services*. Services render as a filterable, tagged card grid (thumbnail, sector
tag, service category, title, short description) with search and relevance sorting, paginated across
58 pages. Every individual service has its **own page and own URL** — "Technical and commercial due
diligence of renewable projects", "Energy market regulation and compliance", "Solar due diligence"
are each independently addressable. The footer offers a second navigation axis by *service type*
(Advisory, Certification, Classification) and by *content type* (Articles, Reports, Cases,
Publications, Videos).

**Read.** This is the model. In a services business, **the service page is the product page.** It is
the unit of SEO, the unit of sales collateral, the URL pasted into an email, and the unit of
analytics. DNV treats each service with the care Waaree gives each module SKU. Vedanjay's legacy site
does the exact opposite — it collapses six services into one URL behind a jQuery toggle.

**Take (defining decision of this project):** **one indexable page per service line**, each with
scope, process, deliverables, evidence and its own enquiry CTA. Plus: breadcrumbs on every inner
page; a taxonomy that lets one project or article surface under multiple services; a second
navigation axis by audience segment.

## B2. Arup, Mott MacDonald, AFRY — engineering-consultancy editorial craft

*(Arup's own site returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch; this reference is included on established
practice in the engineering-consultancy category rather than fresh capture, and is marked as such.)*

The consistent pattern across premium engineering consultancies, and the one that separates them
visually from the energy sector: **projects are treated as editorial case studies, not database
rows.** A named project, a photograph of the actual built work, the client's problem, the technical
approach, the measured outcome, and the named engineers who did it. Large type, generous whitespace,
restrained colour, photography of real work rather than stock landscapes.

**Take:** Vedanjay has 52 real works at real substations. Converting even **four** of them into
photographed, written case studies would put it ahead of every Indian firm in its category — none of
which do this.

# Cross-cutting observations

**1. The sector has converged on teal.** Tata Power (teal / sea-green), Suzlon (teal / turquoise),
ReNew (teal-green on white). Vikram Solar uses red, Waaree blue/red. A teal-green Vedanjay would be
visually indistinguishable from its three largest neighbours — and its legacy mid-green (#47A859)
sits inside that same convergence zone. This is a **colour-differentiation opportunity**, addressed
in `docs/03-design/color-system.md`.

**2. Everyone leads with capacity; nobody leads with competence.** Every one of the five opens on a
GW/MW number. Vedanjay cannot and should not compete on portfolio scale — and does not need to. Its
proof is *regulatory access* (QCA in three states, 'A' class licence, CEIG/SLDC approvals) and
*repeat business from the very companies that own those gigawatts*.

**3. Stock photography is universal.** All five rely heavily on turbines-against-blue-sky and
panel-rows-at-golden-hour. Real photographs of a 132 kV bay, a telemetry cabinet or an engineer at an
SLDC terminal would be immediately, visibly different.

**4. Nobody names the buyer.** Every site addresses "the energy transition" in the abstract. None
says *"you are a factory paying ₹9/unit on an HT connection"*. Vedanjay's legacy segmentation
(Industry / Commercial / Utilities / RE Generators) is a better instinct than anything in Group A —
it just needs to be built out rather than left as four homepage tiles.

**5. The one metric that transfers is Suzlon's.** Not 22,000 MW — **1,900+ industrial clients.** A
count of clients served is the natural proof for a services firm, and Vedanjay has ~41 named ones.
