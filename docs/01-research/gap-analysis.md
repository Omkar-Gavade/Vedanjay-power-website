## 2. What competitors do poorly

| Weakness | Where | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Interchangeable slogan headlines | Waaree, Vikram Solar | "Accelerating Global Energy Transition" would fit any of the five — the H1 does no work |
| Rotating hero carousels | Vikram Solar, legacy Vedanjay | Slides 2–5 largely unseen; every extra slide dilutes the first |
| Undated statistics | Waaree, Suzlon, Tata Power | Unfalsifiable numbers get discounted by exactly the sophisticated readers they target |
| Homepage as sitemap | Tata Power (15 sections) | Nothing is emphasised when everything is included |
| Logo walls with no context | All five | Prove contact, not outcome, and not satisfaction |
| Stock photography monoculture | All five | Turbines against blue sky, panels at golden hour — visually interchangeable |
| Abstract address ("the energy transition") | All five | Nobody speaks to the individual buyer's actual situation |
| Projects as data, not narrative | All five, and legacy Vedanjay | Tables and counts instead of demonstrated capability |

## 3. What most competitors all look like

Researching five sector leaders produced a strikingly uniform template:

```
Teal or green brand colour on white
  ↓
Hero: drone footage or stock photograph of turbines/panels
  ↓
H1: an abstract statement about the energy transition
  ↓
A GW / MW capacity number, undated
  ↓
Product or business-unit card grid
  ↓
Sustainability / ESG commitment with a net-zero year
  ↓
Client logo wall
  ↓
News carousel
  ↓
Careers CTA
  ↓
Multi-column footer
```

Three of the five use a teal-family palette. Four of five lead with a capacity figure. Five of five
use stock renewable-energy photography. Five of five address the reader in the abstract.

**This uniformity is the opportunity.** In a category this converged, restraint and specificity read
as premium, because everything around them is generic and loud.

## 4. The structural insight

The five named comparators are **asset owners and manufacturers**. Vedanjay is a **services firm**.
Their proof is *what they own*; Vedanjay's proof is *what it is licensed to do and who keeps
re-hiring it*.

| | Asset owner / manufacturer | Vedanjay (services) |
|---|---|---|
| Core proof | GW owned, units shipped | Regulatory access; repeat mandates |
| Headline metric | Capacity, countries | Clients served, projects executed, approvals secured |
| IA unit | Product / business unit | **Service line** |
| Buyer question | "Can you supply at scale?" | "Can you get my plant approved, connected and scheduled?" |
| Credibility source | Balance sheet, listing | Licences, named utility clients, repeat business |
| Right model | Waaree, ReNew, Suzlon | **DNV** |

Copying Group A's IA would force Vedanjay to compete on the one axis where it is weakest, and hide
the three axes where it is genuinely strong.

## 6. Differentiation strategy

### Position

> **Vedanjay Power is the firm that gets renewable energy projects approved, connected, metered and
> scheduled — and keeps them running.**

Not a generator. Not a manufacturer. Not an "energy transition partner". The specialist that asset
owners hire to navigate the regulatory and grid-interface layer between a built plant and a
revenue-earning one.

Note that the five largest names in the sector already agree with this positioning — they hire
Vedanjay to do exactly this.

### Five differentiation moves

**1. Compete on access, not scale.**
Everyone leads with GW. Vedanjay leads with **QCA in 3 states · 'A' class electrical licence ·
CEIG / SLDC / DISCOM approvals secured**. This is a lane no comparator occupies, because for
them it is a cost centre and for Vedanjay it is the product.

**2. Make repeat business the hero metric.**
"Suzlon has returned seven times. MPPTCL seven times. ReNew five times." No competitor
publishes this. It is already true in Vedanjay's data. It is the most persuasive fact the company
owns, and it currently appears nowhere.

**3. Photograph the real work.**
Every comparator uses stock. Vedanjay has 52 executed works at real substations — 132 kV bays,
telemetry cabinets, 33 kV metering yards, capacitor banks, SLDC terminals. Real photography of
unglamorous technical work will look categorically different from turbines-against-blue-sky, and
signals *we were actually there*. **This is the highest-impact single investment in the project.**
(Dependent on `TO VERIFY` #19.)

**4. Speak to the buyer, not to the transition.**
Segment pages that open on the reader's situation. An HT industrial buyer wants a lower tariff, not
a fossil-free world. Vedanjay's existing four-segment split is a better instinct than anything
observed in Group A; it needs building out.

**5. Look engineered, not ecological.**
Reject the sector's teal-green-on-white convergence. A graphite-and-deep-green ground with a
**copper** accent (the metal of conductors, busbars and windings) reads as electrical engineering
rather than eco-marketing, and is worn by nobody in this set. Full reasoning in
`docs/03-design/color-system.md`.

### Where Vedanjay must not compete

- **Scale.** No GW claims. It owns no generation.
- **Investor relations.** Private limited company; an IR section would be pretence.
- **Manufacturing.** No products.
- **National-brand emotion.** Tata's register is unavailable to a 15-year-old specialist firm, and imitating it would read as hollow.
- **Sustainability leadership claims.** Without measured, audited data, ESG language is noise. State the honest contribution — enabling others' renewable assets to connect and operate — and stop there.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Contradictory statistics ship as-is (100 vs 110 MW; 700 vs 30 MW) | **High** — destroys credibility with the exact readers targeted | Content freeze blocked on `TO VERIFY` #1–#4. Design degrades gracefully to three stats if one cannot be evidenced |
| No real photography available; client cannot supply site images | **High** — differentiator #3 collapses | Fallback direction: technical-diagram-led art direction (single-line diagrams, grid schematics, state maps) as a deliberate system rather than a shortfall. Still differentiated; less powerful |
| QCA registration lapsed or narrower than claimed | **High** — it is the lead credential | Blocked on `TO VERIFY` #5 before hero copy is finalised |
| Client logo permissions not obtainable | Medium — logo wall and case studies both affected | Fall back to unbranded descriptors ("a listed wind OEM", "a state transmission utility"); the project register text is factual and stands on its own |
| enercast partnership lapsed | Medium | Blocked on `TO VERIFY` #7; forecasting page is written to stand without it |
| Leadership will not supply photos/bios | Medium — weakens the "technocrat" claim | Design leadership cards to work with initials-monogram placeholders, but flag it as a real credibility cost |
| Awards all pre-2019 | Low-medium | Present as a dated Recognition timeline; honesty outperforms a stale ticker |
| Content maintenance stops after launch (as it did in 2017) | **High** — the failure mode that produced the current site | Content lives in structured data with an "as at" date per record; Insights deferred until an owner is named; CMS path documented in `docs/04-architecture/data-flow.md` |
| Over-engineering the frontend for a ~15-page site | Medium | Architecture deliberately excludes Redux, GraphQL and SSR — see `docs/05-decisions/decision-log.md` |

## 8. The one-sentence test

If a visitor lands on the new homepage and reads only the first screen, they should be able to say:

> *"This is an Indian firm, licensed as a QCA in three states, that gets renewable projects through
> CEIG, DISCOM and SLDC approval and keeps them scheduled and maintained — and Suzlon, Tata Power
> Solar, Vikram Solar and ReNew all keep hiring them."*

No competitor's homepage supports a sentence anywhere near this specific. That specificity is the
differentiation strategy.
